# Reference style notes — adapted digest

This condenses the workspace’s `reference_scan_style_notes.md`. It describes sampled observations, not a universal JLPT style manual. Exact approvals and exclusions belong to [the registry](../user_approved_reference_scans.md); public unit records own current editorial choices. Earlier observation IDs, crops, and research runs mentioned in historical notes are not all packaged here.

## Common

The reviewed modern headings consistently used `選びなさい` for N1/N2, `えらびなさい` for N3, and `えらんでください` for N4/N5 across the sampled tasks. The sampled dates were N1 December 2023 and July/December 2025; N2 July 2018, December 2023/2024, and July/December 2025; N3 December 2023/2024 and July/December 2025; N4 December 2024 and July/December 2025; N5 July/December 2025. They do not prove every older instruction, punctuation choice, ruby pattern, or worked example.

Spelling depends on context and function. `子供` and `子ども` coexist within one N1 sitting; N2 animal prose has `いぬ`/`ねこ`, while an N1 cloze has `ネコ`. Observed contractions and author wording should not be normalized simply because another spelling seems more formal or easier. Frequency and absence from a small sample cannot establish historical print.

Ruby can cover only part of a word and can repeat selectively. Underlines have specific endpoints; circled reference numbers can be outside them, and some underlined targets have no reference number. A STAR in a grammar task differs from a star marking eligibility in a notice. Tables, note links, and printed `右のページ` retain meaning even in scrolling HTML.

Always compare pixels with the live rich markup. Prior apparent omissions included an underline omitted by an excerpt, ruby already present in the editorial, and a comma not actually printed. Such false comparisons are not evidence that current content needs correction.

## Level and task limits

N4/N5 vocabulary scaffolds can use `ひとつ` while grammar/reading use `一つ`; “lower level” does not justify changing every kanji to kana. N5 distractors can intentionally use wrong characters. N3’s worked STAR example requires its actual fragment order, star position, and answer marking, not just a generic heading. N2 July 2018’s retyped insert and missing original STAR example are outside approved-page calibration.

The five retained departures from approved print are indexed at the top of the registry. Their accepted editorial status does not convert the chosen wording into observed print or authorize a blanket spelling/grammar change elsewhere. Modern patterns must not be projected onto older Levels 1–4 without period-specific evidence.
